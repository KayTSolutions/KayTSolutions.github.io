Design, in progress

A VR physio game that teaches itself

Physiotherapy only works if patients keep doing their exercises at home. Our team is making those exercises into a mixed-reality fair, and my job is making sure anyone can start playing with no one there to explain.

Client
Saxion XR Lab and Ambient Intelligence, with Roessingh rehabilitation centre
My role
Game designer: UI, onboarding and research
When
September 2026 until early 2027
Platform
Meta Quest 3, passthrough and hand tracking, Unity

The challenge

Players see a small fair in their own living room. Every stall is a game built on one exercise chosen by their therapist: hitting, avoiding or following targets in the space around them.

Our players are Dutch adults aged 40 to 70, and many have never used VR. They play at home, alone. If the first five minutes are confusing, they take the headset off and the therapy stops there.

My research question: how can a zero-instruction onboarding experience be designed so first-time adult and elderly users can learn and correctly perform the exercises without help?

How I’m approaching it

  • Learning from existing games. I analysed how Ring Fit Adventure, HoloMoves, Beat Saber and Dance Central teach movement to people who don’t play games.
  • Designing for real people. Personas from a competitive 42-year-old gamer to a 66-year-old who has never held a controller helped us set defaults and settings.
  • Showing instead of telling. The onboarding is a short flow of screens with a friendly guide character. Players learn by doing one movement at a time, with as little text as possible.
  • Always one gesture away from help. A ticket on the player’s wrist is the pause button on every screen, so nobody gets stuck.
  • Testing in small rounds. Each version is tested with three or four first-time players who get no help. I watch where they hesitate and how long it takes to do the first exercise correctly, then change the design.

Principles

  • Playable without explanation
  • The exercise is the game, so the therapy stays correct
  • Always achievable, whatever the player’s level
  • Safety comes first: the player’s real room stays visible
